**Camera Movement:**
| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Movement Type | Dolly forward |
| Speed | 0.4 m/sec |
| Direction | Straight forward toward the phone |

**Phone Movement:**
| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Movement Type | Gimbal rotation |
| Speed | 15 degrees per second |
| Direction | Turning to show all sides |

**Dual motion:** The camera advances while the phone turns. This combination creates the most dynamic shot in the sequence — the viewer feels both the approach of the camera and the turning of the phone.

**Over 4 seconds:**

- Phone rotates approximately 60 degrees
- The phone turns to show front, side, back, and back-to-front

**No handheld movement. No shake. No vibration.** Both the dolly and the gimbal are precision-controlled. The camera tracks the phone's rotation.

## 7. COMPOSITION

**Framing:** Dynamic center. The phone is positioned in the center of the frame, but the composition anticipates the phone's movement — there is leading room in the direction the phone is turning.

**Grid:** Leading room. The camera leads the phone's direction of movement. As the phone turns, the camera follows, maintaining the phone in the center while providing space in the direction of motion.

**Angle:** Low to eye-level. The camera is positioned slightly below eye level, looking up at the phone. This gives the phone monumentality.

**Aspect ratio:** 2.39:1 (CinemaScope). The wide frame allows the phone to be large while providing spatial context.

**Leading room composition:** The phone should be positioned in the frame so that there is more space in front of it (in the direction of rotation) than behind it. This creates dynamic tension.
